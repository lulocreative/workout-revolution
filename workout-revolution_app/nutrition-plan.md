# Workout Revolution 2.0 | Food & Recovery
**Personal nutrition module, 25 November 2026.** Flexible 7-day example for one adult. Portions are examples, not a diet prescription. All nutrition values are approximate.

## What this is built for
Strength training 3 days most weeks, optional fourth chest/arms session. Main priorities: muscle gain, a gradually leaner waist, stable energy and fewer digestive issues. Last confirmed body weight was 93 kg on 4 August 2026; recent gain of 1–2 kg is user-reported, without a dated measurement. Lactose intolerance is an established constraint.

**Starting target:** generally **140–160 g protein/day** (130 g on an imperfect day is not failure). Three full meals + one flexible protein snack/shake. No required calorie counting. A 25 g portion of dark chocolate can stay. Keep a flexible Saturday meal.

**Food preferences retained from earlier guides:** eggs, pasta with tuna and tomato, chicken, fish, salmon, noodles, rice, potatoes, wholegrain toast, fruit, dark chocolate. Work travel and social meals are normal, not exceptions to punish.

### Important corrections to older plans
- The earlier documents disagree about mandatory 2-scoop shakes, 130–150 g versus 150–190 g protein, and immediate post-workout eating. This module favors a sustainable daily target and **optional one-scoop shake** when useful.
- You do **not** have to eat breakfast within one hour of waking or avoid carbohydrates at dinner. Prioritize enough food around the session, especially given previous exercise dizziness.
- Older claims that sodium or cortisol alone explain belly fat are **not established** by your history. Monitor waist/weight and digestive symptoms rather than assuming a single cause.
- Lactose-free dairy is now the default. Some older documents recommended standard Greek yogurt, cottage cheese and whey without addressing intolerance.

## Easy portion method, without calorie tracking
- **Protein:** 1–2 palms at each main meal (e.g. ~150–180 g raw chicken, ~170 g salmon, ~200 g skyr plus eggs).
- **Carbs:** 1 fist cooked rice/pasta/potatoes as a base, add a second if training hard, hungry or losing weight too fast. Recipes show dry weights so you can cook them reproducibly.
- **Vegetables:** around 1–2 fists at lunch and dinner, using whatever you tolerate.
- **Fats:** a measured spoon of olive oil or a small handful of nuts is enough for most meals.
- **Training window:** meal with protein + carbs 1–3 h before lifting; if hungry after a long gap, add banana + skyr or another small snack. A protein-rich dinner within the following few hours is sufficient.
- **Rest day:** similar protein, carbs according to appetite. No special rest-day protein cut.
- **Fluid:** drink regularly, using roughly 2–3 L total fluids as a flexible starting point; add more as needed when sweating. No need to force water to flush sodium.

## Recipes with portions (all for one adult)

### Breakfast

#### Eggs, rye toast and lactose-free skyr  
**12 min · ~38 g protein · ~525 kcal (estimate)**
**Ingredients (1 serving):** 2 medium eggs eggs; 2 thick slices, 90 g wholegrain rye bread; 150 g lactose-free high-protein skyr; 120 g tomatoes; 1 tsp olive oil.
**Method:** 1. Scramble or fry the eggs gently in the oil. 2. Toast the rye bread; slice the tomato alongside. 3. Eat the skyr on the side or save it for later in the morning.
**Notes:** A substantial breakfast without a huge shake.
**Swap:** Replace skyr with lactose-free cottage cheese if preferred.

#### Overnight oats with skyr and berries  
**5 min · ~41 g protein · ~600 kcal (estimate)**
**Ingredients (1 serving):** 65 g oats; 200 g lactose-free high-protein skyr; 200 ml lactose-free milk; 100 g frozen berries; 10 g unsalted nuts.
**Method:** 1. Stir oats, lactose-free milk and skyr in a jar. 2. Refrigerate overnight. Add berries and nuts before eating.
**Notes:** Use lactose-free products; regular Greek yogurt may cause symptoms.
**Swap:** Make with fortified soy milk and a suitable high-protein dairy-free yogurt.
**Batch prep:** Prepare two jars at once; keep chilled.

#### Eggs with chicken and rye toast  
**12 min · ~40 g protein · ~500 kcal (estimate)**
**Ingredients (1 serving):** 2 medium eggs eggs; 60 g leftover cooked chicken cooked chicken breast, leftover; 2 slices, 80 g wholegrain rye bread; 100 g cucumber; 1 tsp olive oil.
**Method:** 1. Warm the cooked chicken in a pan until piping hot. 2. Cook two eggs; serve on rye toast with sliced cucumber.
**Notes:** Use cooked chicken stored safely in the fridge; this is not raw weight.
**Swap:** Replace leftover chicken with smoked tofu or another cooked lean protein.

#### Banana, oat and peanut-butter smoothie  
**5 min · ~47 g protein · ~650 kcal (estimate)**
**Ingredients (1 serving):** 1 scoop, 30 g powder lactose-free whey isolate or tolerated protein powder; 250 ml lactose-free milk; 55 g oats; 1 medium banana banana; 1 tbsp, 15 g peanut butter.
**Method:** 1. Blend the milk, protein powder, oats, banana and peanut butter until smooth. 2. Add water or ice to adjust texture.
**Notes:** A meal, not a compulsory post-workout shake. Check the powder is lactose-free or personally tolerated.
**Swap:** Replace peanut butter with 15 g nuts or seed butter.

### Lunch

#### Tuna and tomato pasta  
**18 min · ~50 g protein · ~700 kcal (estimate)**
**Ingredients (1 serving):** 110 g dry dry pasta; 1 drained can, 120 g canned tuna in water, drained; 180 g tomato passata; 80 g spinach; 2 tsp olive oil.
**Method:** 1. Boil pasta in salted water. 2. Warm passata and spinach in a pan with olive oil. 3. Fold in drained tuna at the end, toss with pasta and season with lemon, pepper and herbs.
**Notes:** Keep this as a staple. The plan uses canned tuna once per week; vary fish choices.
**Swap:** Replace the tuna with 150 g cooked chicken or tinned salmon.

#### Chicken rice bowl with vegetables  
**25 min · ~46 g protein · ~725 kcal (estimate)**
**Ingredients (1 serving):** 150 g raw chicken chicken breast, raw; 105 g dry rice dry rice; 230 g mixed vegetables (fresh or frozen); 2 tsp olive oil.
**Method:** 1. Cook the rice. 2. Season and pan-cook the chicken until cooked through. 3. Steam or stir-fry vegetables; serve with rice and a little olive oil or lemon.
**Notes:** Make two portions for Tuesday and Friday.
**Swap:** Use potatoes instead of rice; adjust the carb portion to appetite.
**Batch prep:** Cook two portions; refrigerate promptly and reheat once until hot.

#### Chicken and chickpea wholegrain wrap  
**15 min · ~45 g protein · ~525 kcal (estimate)**
**Ingredients (1 serving):** 130 g raw chicken chicken breast, raw; 65 g drained, rinsed canned chickpeas, drained; 1 large wrap, 75 g wholegrain tortilla; 80 g salad greens; 100 g cucumber; 50 g lactose-free yogurt lactose-free plain yogurt.
**Method:** 1. Cook chicken in strips, or use an equivalent portion of safely stored leftover cooked chicken. 2. Rinse chickpeas well. Mix yogurt with lemon, pepper and herbs. 3. Fill wrap with chicken, chickpeas, greens, cucumber and sauce.
**Notes:** If chickpeas cause bloating, omit them and add a little more chicken or rice.
**Swap:** For sensitive digestion use 30 g chickpeas and increase chicken to 170 g.

#### Lean turkey bolognese pasta  
**25 min · ~48 g protein · ~750 kcal (estimate)**
**Ingredients (1 serving):** 150 g raw lean turkey mince lean turkey mince, raw; 105 g dry dry pasta; 200 g tomato passata; 80 g spinach; 2 tsp olive oil.
**Method:** 1. Cook the pasta. 2. Brown turkey mince in olive oil; add passata and simmer 8–10 minutes. 3. Stir in spinach and herbs, toss with pasta.
**Notes:** Make twice; one batch covers Thursday and Sunday lunches.
**Swap:** Use lean beef mince occasionally instead of turkey.
**Batch prep:** Cook two portions, store one chilled promptly.

#### Chicken and avocado rye sandwich  
**10 min · ~51 g protein · ~600 kcal (estimate)**
**Ingredients (1 serving):** 125 g cooked leftover chicken cooked chicken breast, leftover; 3 slices, 110 g wholegrain rye bread; 60 g avocado; 110 g tomatoes; 60 g lettuce.
**Method:** 1. Toast rye lightly. 2. Slice cooked chicken, avocado and tomato. 3. Make a sandwich with lettuce; season with lemon and pepper.
**Notes:** A simple option before a weekend social dinner.
**Swap:** Replace chicken with cooked turkey or 2 eggs plus extra egg whites.

### Dinner

#### One-pan chicken, potatoes and broccoli  
**35 min · ~51 g protein · ~675 kcal (estimate)**
**Ingredients (1 serving):** 160 g raw chicken chicken breast, raw; 380 g raw potatoes potatoes, raw; 220 g broccoli; 1 tbsp olive oil.
**Method:** 1. Heat oven to 210°C. Chop potatoes and toss with half the oil; roast 15 minutes. 2. Add seasoned chicken and broccoli with remaining oil. 3. Roast until chicken is safely cooked and potatoes are tender; typically 15–20 more minutes depending on thickness.
**Notes:** Use herbs, paprika and lemon for flavor.
**Swap:** Swap potatoes for a portion of cooked rice.

#### Salmon with noodles and vegetables  
**25 min · ~47 g protein · ~825 kcal (estimate)**
**Ingredients (1 serving):** 160 g raw fillet salmon fillet, raw; 100 g dry dry noodles; 240 g mixed vegetables (fresh or frozen); 1 tsp olive oil.
**Method:** 1. Bake or pan-sear salmon until cooked through. 2. Cook noodles and steam the vegetables. 3. Combine with lemon, ginger and a small splash of reduced-salt soy sauce if tolerated.
**Notes:** Soy sauce is a seasoning, not something that needs to be eliminated.
**Swap:** Swap noodles for rice or potatoes.

#### Baked cod, potatoes and peas  
**30 min · ~46 g protein · ~625 kcal (estimate)**
**Ingredients (1 serving):** 180 g raw cod cod fillet, raw; 320 g potatoes, raw; 130 g green peas; 1 tbsp olive oil.
**Method:** 1. Boil or roast the potatoes until tender. 2. Bake cod with lemon and pepper at 190°C for about 12–16 minutes, depending on thickness. 3. Heat peas and serve with olive oil.
**Notes:** White fish is lean; the olive oil and potatoes make this satisfying.
**Swap:** Replace cod with pollock, haddock or other white fish.

#### Salmon, potatoes and green beans  
**30 min · ~43 g protein · ~750 kcal (estimate)**
**Ingredients (1 serving):** 160 g raw fillet salmon fillet, raw; 350 g potatoes, raw; 200 g green beans; 2 tsp olive oil.
**Method:** 1. Roast the potatoes at 210°C for about 25–30 minutes. 2. Bake or pan-fry the salmon until cooked through. 3. Steam green beans and dress with olive oil and lemon.
**Notes:** Second salmon meal of the week; no need for an additional fish-oil supplement recommendation here.
**Swap:** Swap green beans for broccoli or carrots.

#### Beef and pepper rice stir-fry  
**20 min · ~44 g protein · ~800 kcal (estimate)**
**Ingredients (1 serving):** 160 g raw lean beef lean beef strips, raw; 105 g dry rice dry rice; 220 g bell peppers; 50 g onion (optional if tolerated); 2 tsp olive oil.
**Method:** 1. Cook rice. 2. Sear beef strips in a hot pan and set aside. 3. Stir-fry sliced peppers and onion in olive oil, then return beef and season with herbs or reduced-salt soy sauce.
**Notes:** If onion triggers bloating, leave it out. This is the only dedicated red-meat meal of the week.
**Swap:** Use chicken or tofu instead of beef.

#### Chicken fajitas with peppers  
**22 min · ~49 g protein · ~700 kcal (estimate)**
**Ingredients (1 serving):** 150 g raw chicken chicken breast, raw; 2 medium wholegrain tortillas, 110 g wholegrain tortilla; 200 g bell peppers; 40 g onion (optional if tolerated); 60 g lactose-free yogurt sauce lactose-free plain yogurt; 2 tsp olive oil.
**Method:** 1. Cut chicken and peppers into strips. 2. Cook chicken thoroughly, then add peppers and optional onion with fajita spices. 3. Serve in warm tortillas with lactose-free yogurt mixed with lemon.
**Notes:** Easy for sharing; use smaller servings of onion if sensitive.
**Swap:** Use rice instead of tortillas; change sauce to guacamole.

#### Flexible social dinner  
**0 min · Varies / deliberately unknown**
**Method:** 1. Choose what you actually want: burger, pizza, restaurant food or home cooking. 2. Include a meaningful protein source and eat until comfortably satisfied. 3. Return to your usual meals at the next opportunity; no compensation diet.
**Notes:** Protein and calories are deliberately not guessed. This meal is user-defined.
**Swap:** Any meal that fits your appetite and social plans.

## Optional additions when the sample plan leaves you hungry
Add a banana, 1–2 slices rye bread, or another 40–60 g **dry** pasta/rice (or 150–200 g potatoes) to a training meal. A 20–30 g portion of dark chocolate after dinner is also fine. If sessions feel flat or body weight falls rapidly, add fuel rather than pushing through hunger. Do not regard the recipe calorie estimates as a rigid ceiling.

## Bloating and belly-fat check, without guessing
For **14 days**: compare morning waist at belly-button level after using the bathroom; measure morning weight 2–3 times/week if willing; log obvious bloating and whether it follows dairy, very large meals, onions, legumes, carbonation or alcohol. Only adjust a suspected trigger after noticing a repeatable pattern. Persistent painful distension, unexplained persistent change in bowel habits, bleeding, vomiting or other worrying symptoms need medical attention.

After 2–3 weeks: if the waist is stable/down and gym performance holds, keep going. If waist and average weight both rise and getting leaner is still the goal, remove one discretionary energy-dense extra or reduce an added carb portion, not your main protein. If energy or strength drops, add a carb serving around training and reassess.

## Grocery prep in under 75 minutes
1. Cook a large pot of rice for two lunch bowls; refrigerate promptly in shallow containers.
2. Cook two chicken portions for lunches and extra for one breakfast/sandwich if possible.
3. Brown two portions of turkey mince with tomato sauce and spinach for Thursday and Sunday.
4. Assemble two overnight-oats jars.
5. Wash vegetables, portion frozen berries and keep whey/skyr ready as an optional convenient snack.
6. Cook fish fresh or from frozen when needed. Follow food storage labels and safe reheating practices.
